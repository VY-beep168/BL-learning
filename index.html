PRONOUNS
What is a pronoun?

A pronoun is a small word that takes the place of a name, so we don't say the same name again and again.

Tom is eight. He is my brother.
Mum and Dad are kind. They are at home.

I is the only one that gets am.

One person or thing (he, she, it) gets is.

You and lots of people or things (we, they) get are.

It works for names too: My brother is eight. My parents are kind.

Short forms

We can squash the two words into one. The apostrophe (') takes the place of the missing letter.

I am → I'm he is → he's she is → she's it is → it's you are → you're we are → we're they are → they're
SAYING "NOT"

To say something is NOT true, put not after the be verb.

Iamnota teacher.
→I'm not a teacher.
Heisnothere.
→He isn't here.
Theyarenothere.
→They aren't here.
Watch out!

is not and are not are TWO words. Leave a gap between them.

arenot   are not
isnt   is'nt   isn't

In isn't and aren't, the apostrophe sits where the o of "not" went.
